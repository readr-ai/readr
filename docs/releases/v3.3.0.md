# v3.3.0 — Recap, one tap away

v3.2.2 made the spoiler-free recap honest: Ask sees only what you have read.
It was still a chip inside a panel you had to open first. This release puts
the recap where a reader coming back to a book actually looks — the reader's
toolbar and the Continue Reading card — and has the answer arrive already
asked, with a line saying where it stops.

## What to test

**Reader.** Open an EPUB you are partway through. Next to Listen and Ask
there is a Recap button (⇧⌘R on a Mac). Press it: the Ask panel opens with
"Recap what I've read so far — no spoilers" already in the transcript as
sent, a "Recap up to where you are" line above it reading like
"Chapter 7 of 24 · 31% · The Whale", and the answer streaming in. It names
nothing from later chapters. Press Done, press Ask: a plain Ask, nothing
pre-sent.

**Native PDF.** Open a PDF shown as original pages: no Recap button. Switch
to the reading view in Appearance: the button appears.

**Home.** On Home, each Continue Reading card for a text book shows
"Chapter N of M" under the Continue pill and a ✦ Recap link beside it. Tap
Recap: the book opens at your place, and a beat later the Ask panel opens
with the recap already sent. PDF cards show neither. Cards in the same row
stay level with one another whether or not they carry the line.

**No provider.** Disconnect every provider, press Recap: the panel opens on
"No AI provider connected". Connect a key from there and press Done: the
recap sends itself.

**Mac, book already open.** With a reader window open behind the library,
tap the card's Recap: the existing window comes forward and opens the recap
rather than a second window opening.

## New

- **Recap button in the reader.** A Recap item in the reader toolbar on
  iPhone, iPad and Mac (⇧⌘R), beside Listen and Ask, opens Ask with the
  recap question already sent. The panel's header says where the recap stops
  — chapter N of M, percent through the book, and the chapter's title — so
  you can see at a glance that it covers the right stretch. Only text books
  get the button: a native PDF page is not a reading position.
- **Recap from the library.** Continue Reading cards on Home show
  "Chapter N of M" under the Continue pill and a ✦ Recap action that opens
  the book and then the recap.

## Under the hood

- `ReadingPositionSummary` (ReadrKit, tested) turns a `Book` and a
  `ReadingFrontier` into the "where am I" line: chapter title with a
  TOC-then-"Chapter N" fallback, "Chapter N of M" over linear chapters only,
  and percent by character count, clamped. Empty books and books of only
  non-linear chapters yield nil; image-only books fall back to whole
  chapters.
- `AskPanelView` takes an `initialQuestion` and a `positionCaption`. The
  question is a plain `let` handed to `AskViewModel.sendInitialQuestionIfReady`
  from `.task(id:)` and on provider change; the view model keeps a once-flag.
  It is not captured at init: on the iPhone simulator both a sheet's
  `@State(initialValue:)` and the `@StateObject`'s init argument arrived nil
  while the view's `let`s carried the presented values.
- `AppModel.pendingRecapBookID` is the one piece of plumbing between the
  card and the reader.
- Two UI tests cover the reader button and the card action against the
  stubbed provider (`testRecapButtonOpensAskWithTheRecapAlreadySent`,
  `testContinueReadingRecapOpensTheBookIntoARecap`).
