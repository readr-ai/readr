# v3.3.0 — Readr Voice is back on iPhone and iPad

3.2.2 switched Readr Voice off on every current iOS build: Kokoro through
CoreML crashes the process inside Apple's BNNS runtime on iOS 26.4 and later
(FluidAudio#817/#844, three App Store crash logs), the crash cannot be caught,
and no compute-unit routing avoids it. This release brings the voice back by
changing the runtime, not the model: on iPhone and iPad Readr Voice now runs
the same Kokoro-82M on the Metal GPU through MLX, which never touches BNNS.
iOS is MLX or an Apple voice — CoreML is never built there. The Mac keeps its
CoreML engine and its OS gate.

## What to test

All of this needs a real iPhone or iPad — the simulator cannot run MLX at all,
and CI only proves the code compiles. The full list, with what to record, is
section 9 of `docs/DEVICE-SMOKE-TEST.md`.

**First Listen (iOS 26.4 or later, fresh install).** Open Alice's Adventures
in Wonderland and press Listen. An Apple voice starts within a second; the
voice menu shows Readr Voice checked as the first row, a "downloading" note,
and the locked-screen note. Within a few minutes on Wi-Fi the voice changes
to Readr Voice at a sentence boundary with no gap, repeat, or skipped
sentence, and the downloading note disappears. The first Readr Voice
sentence should start as promptly as the later ones — the engine speaks a
warm-up sentence to itself after the load.

**Half an hour.** Leave it reading. No crash, no stall, no sentence spoken
twice; Xcode's Debug Memory gauge stays under 1GB and does not climb.

**Lock mid-sentence.** Press the side button while Readr Voice is
mid-sentence. The sentence finishes (or the Apple voice re-reads it), the
next sentence is an Apple voice, and playback continues from the lock
screen. Unlock and return to the app: the next sentence is Readr Voice
again. The app must not have quit — check Settings › Privacy & Security ›
Analytics & Improvements › Analytics Data for any `Readr-` log.

**Control Center, a banner, a call.** Pull down Control Center while Readr
Voice is reading, then dismiss it; let a notification banner arrive; take a
short call. Readr Voice keeps reading through all three — only the lock
hands over to an Apple voice. On an iPad, put another app beside Readr in
Split View and type in it: Readr Voice keeps reading.

**Lock during the first download.** Fresh install, press Listen, lock the
phone before the download finishes and leave it locked well past the point
it must have completed. The Apple voice keeps reading; nothing crashes.
Unlock: Readr Voice takes over within a few sentences.

**Pause and resume, skips, a chapter crossing, speed changes** — all in Readr
Voice, all continuing from where they should.

**Mac.** Nothing changes: Readr Voice is still the first row of the voice
menu on macOS 26.6+ and still absent on 26.4–26.5. The Mac app no longer
carries MLX at all (project.yml `platformFilter: iOS`), so the DMG should be
the same size as 3.2.2's.

## New

- **Readr Voice is offered and defaulted again on iOS.** An English book
  with no stored voice choice narrates in Readr Voice; a stored Readr Voice
  pick, kept through 3.2.2, comes back on its own. Apple's voices remain a
  pick away.
- **First Listen downloads, then switches.** The first English-book Listen
  fetches ~330MB of MLX weights and ~60MB of voice packs
  (`mlx-community/Kokoro-82M-bf16`) and ~20MB of pronunciation assets
  (`beshkenadze/kitten-tts-g2p`) from huggingface.co — PRIVACY.md discloses
  both, and that the download library keeps a cache plus a working copy, so
  the footprint can approach twice that until iOS purges the cache.
  Narration starts immediately in an Apple voice and switches to Readr Voice
  at a sentence boundary once the model is in, exactly as the Mac has done
  since 3.2.0.
- **With the screen locked, an Apple voice reads.** Metal withholds the GPU
  from a backgrounded app (mlx-swift#274), so from the moment the app is
  backgrounded the next sentence goes to the Apple voice; Readr Voice
  returns at the next sentence after you come back. The voice menu says so
  in one line. Control Center, notification banners, incoming calls and an
  iPad Split View neighbour do not count as backgrounded and do not change
  the voice. Every other control — pause, resume, skip, chapters,
  lock-screen and headphone buttons — works as before.
- **Pronunciation without espeak or CoreML.** The MLX engine uses the
  package's Misaki lexicon (gold + silver) with a 3MB BART fallback on MLX
  for names and rare words, so nothing is silently dropped and nothing runs
  on the crashing path.

## Under the hood

- `NarrationEnginePolicy` (ReadrKit, every row table-tested) decides per
  sentence which engine speaks — platform voice, CoreML Kokoro on macOS, or
  MLX Kokoro on iOS when it is ready *and* the app is in the foreground —
  and which runtime a platform prepares. `RoutingSpeechEngine` builds only
  the engine the policy names (CoreML on macOS outside the BNNS gate, MLX on
  an iOS device with a Metal GPU, neither otherwise), so iOS never
  constructs CoreML Kokoro even on the iOS 18–26.3 builds that pass the gate.
- `SentenceAudioPlayer` is the one `AVAudioPlayer` contract both Kokoro
  engines share — synthesis gap reported as `.speaking`, `player.isPlaying`
  read rather than a flag, a refused `play()` reported as a failure, state
  cleared before every callback. A punctuation-only "sentence" that yields
  no samples finishes at once instead of handing AVAudioPlayer a 44-byte WAV.
- `MLXKokoroSpeechEngine`: "foreground" means not backgrounded
  (`didEnterBackground` → `willEnterForeground`) with a 1.5s head start on
  `willResignActive` for the lock; a first-use download that lands with the
  phone locked waits for the foreground on a continuation, not a poll, and
  the actor re-checks the flag immediately before the first Metal call. A
  synthesis that runs past 30s is abandoned through ReadrKit's
  `raceAgainstDeadline` (the old task-group timeout could never fire — a
  group awaits every child, and MLX synthesis cannot be cancelled) and the
  engine marks itself failed so later sentences go straight to the Apple
  voice; re-picking the voice retries. A download that fails while
  backgrounded resets to not-ready and retries on the next foreground
  sentence. After the load the engine speaks "Ready." to itself once, so the
  first real sentence carries no lexicon parse or kernel compile. The
  download is checked for the weights, config and voice pack the package's
  own top-level completeness check does not look at, with one clear-and-retry.
  MLX's GPU cache is capped at 64MB (the free buffer pool, not resident
  memory). `ClauseSplitter` now prefers a clause break only within 40% of
  the target, else the nearest space, so "Well, " plus a long run no longer
  yields a one-word head.
- project.yml: the four MLX/HuggingFace products carry `platformFilter: iOS`,
  so the Mac app neither links MLX nor compiles mlx-swift's Metal shaders;
  release.yml drops the Metal toolchain step. The iOS build carries the
  Increased Memory Limit entitlement, and testflight.yml now signs the
  archive with cloud signing (an unsigned archive carried no entitlements)
  and fails the lane if `increased-memory-limit` is missing from the
  archived app. Dependencies: Blaizzy/mlx-audio-swift at `d20cbd660424`
  (MIT), ml-explore/mlx-swift 0.31.6 (MIT), swift-huggingface 0.10.0.
  Decision memo: `docs/research/MLX-KOKORO-IOS.md`.
