# v3.3.1 — Readr Voice is the only voice you hear

3.3.0 brought Readr Voice back to iPhone and iPad, but it kept two habits
from the days when the model had to be downloaded and could not run with the
screen locked: an Apple voice read while the model was on its way, and an
Apple voice read whenever the phone was locked. Readers heard the voice
change under them, and the voice menu was a wall of Apple voices with Readr
Voice at the top. This release makes Readr Voice the narrator, full stop:
the first Listen waits for it rather than substituting; the voice menu is
Readr Voice with the Apple voices behind a disclosure; sentences are
synthesized ahead of the voice into a buffer on disk, which is what plays
with the screen locked while the CPU refills it; and an Apple voice is heard
only when a reader picks one (or the book is not in English).

## What to test

All of the locked-screen and buffer steps need a real iPhone or iPad — the
simulator cannot run MLX at all, and CI proves only that the code compiles.
Record the device model and iOS version with the results. Section 9 of
`docs/DEVICE-SMOKE-TEST.md` has the same steps with what to write down.

**First Listen (iPhone or iPad on iOS 26.4 or later, fresh install).** Open
Alice's Adventures in Wonderland and press Listen. Nothing speaks. The
middle of the Listen bar reads "Preparing Readr Voice… 12% of 410MB, once"
with a progress line that climbs while the weights download, then a small
spinner while the pronunciation assets come in and the model loads; the
play/pause control shows Pause. When the model is in — a minute or two on
Wi-Fi — the first sentence is Readr Voice, and the bar shows the sentence.
No Apple voice at any point, no sentence read twice, none skipped.

**Pause during the wait.** Press pause while "Preparing" shows: the bar
flips to Play and the download carries on. Press play: "Preparing" is back,
and the first sentence arrives in Readr Voice when the model does. The lock
screen shows a pause control during the wait, and its pause/play do the
same.

**The download fails.** Fresh install, Wi-Fi off, press Listen: the bar
reads "Readr Voice couldn't download." with a Retry link, the play control
shows Play, and nothing speaks. Turn Wi-Fi on and press Retry: preparing,
then Readr Voice. Pressing Play instead of Retry does the same. Open the
voice menu during the failure: a note says Retry is on the bar.

**The buffer fills.** With Readr Voice reading in the foreground, watch the
right of the bar: within a minute a small "1 min ready" appears and climbs
— "12 min ready", "48 min ready" — as sentences are synthesized ahead on
the CPU. It stops climbing around an hour (the horizon). Plug the phone
in: it climbs again, to the end of the book or two hours, whichever comes
first. Unplug: it stops adding but keeps what it has. Skip back a sentence:
instant, no synthesis gap. Change speed to 1.5×: the sentence continues
faster from where it is, no restart, and the figure does not drop — the
buffer is at 1× and the player applies the speed.

**Lock the screen, thirty minutes.** With "20 min ready" or more, lock the
phone. Readr Voice keeps reading from the lock screen — the same voice, no
gap at the sentence boundary. Leave it locked for thirty minutes with
Xcode attached: no crash, no stall, the lock screen's pause/play and
headphone controls work. Unlock: still Readr Voice, no sentence heard
twice. In Settings › Readr › Report a bug (or Xcode's console) the
diagnostics log has lines like `Readr Voice (MLX) sentence 40: cpu 6210ms
for 5120ms of audio (0.8× realtime); cpu rtf over last 5: 0.85; 1720s
ahead` — the first `cpu` line marks the switch, then one line in ten.
**Write down the cpu rtf figures**, the peak memory from Xcode's Debug
navigator during the half hour (it must stay under 1GB and not climb), and
how far the "ahead" figure fell or rose while locked.

**Lock with nothing ready.** Fresh install; press Listen and lock the phone
as soon as the first sentence is heard, so almost nothing is buffered. The
next sentence either arrives in Readr Voice after a CPU synthesis (if the
CPU has been keeping up) or narration pauses at the sentence boundary: the
lock screen's second line reads "Unlock Readr to keep listening", a
notification with the same words arrives (the first Listen asked for
permission; if you declined, there is no notification and it simply
pauses), and there is no Apple voice. Unlock and open Readr: narration
resumes on the same sentence in Readr Voice by itself, the notification is
gone, and the bar's middle briefly showed "Unlock Readr to keep listening"
before the sentence line came back.

**Relaunch.** Force-quit Readr, reopen, press Listen at the same place:
the sentence starts at once in Readr Voice, before the model has loaded
(the buffer is on disk), and "N min ready" shows what survived.

**The voice menu, English book.** Open it while Readr Voice is reading:
one voice row, "Readr Voice", checked, a "Keeps reading with the screen
locked" note, and an "Other voices…" submenu holding the Apple voices for
the book's language. Pick one there: it reads from the next word, and the
menu shows Readr Voice unchecked with the Apple voice checked inside the
submenu; the "ready" figure disappears (the buffer is Readr Voice's). Pick
Readr Voice again: it returns, and so does the figure.

**The voice menu, a non-English book.** Open a French EPUB (or a book
whose language tag isn't English): the Apple voices for its language are
listed directly, as in 3.3.0, and no Readr Voice row is offered.

**Delete a book.** Delete Alice from the library and check
Settings › General › iPhone Storage › Readr: its Readr Voice audio went
with it.

**Mac (macOS 26.6 or later).** The same first-Listen wait — "Preparing
Readr Voice… 104MB, once" with a spinner (the Mac's download reports no
progress) — then Readr Voice from the first sentence. The voice menu has
the same shape as on iOS without the locked-screen note; no "ready"
figure (the Mac synthesizes as it goes). On macOS 26.4–26.5 nothing
changes: Readr Voice is not offered, an Apple voice reads, and the menu
says why.

## New

- **Scanned PDFs open as pages.** When a PDF has no text layer, Readr shows
  its original pages and says that Ask, Listen, search, highlights, and the
  Reading view need text.
- **The first Listen waits for Readr Voice.** Where Readr Voice can run,
  an English book's first Listen shows "Preparing Readr Voice…" in the
  Listen bar — with the download's progress on iPhone and iPad, an
  indeterminate spinner and the size elsewhere — and narration starts the
  moment the model is in. No Apple voice reads meanwhile. If the download
  fails the bar says so and offers Retry; still no Apple voice unless the
  reader picks one.
- **Readr Voice only, for English books.** The voice menu shows Readr
  Voice, checked, and an "Other voices…" disclosure with the Apple voices
  behind it — kept for readers who want them (an accessibility voice set
  up in Settings still narrates and still shows checked in there). Books in
  other languages keep the Apple list as before. The "downloading —
  switching automatically" and "with the screen locked, an Apple voice
  reads" notes are gone; iPhone and iPad say "Keeps reading with the
  screen locked" instead.
- **Readr Voice reads ahead.** On iPhone and iPad the sentences after the
  one being read are synthesized on the CPU, whether the app is open or
  not — an hour ahead, or the rest of the book while the phone is
  charging — and kept on disk as compressed audio (about 30MB an hour, two
  hours at most, oldest first, gone with the book when it is deleted). The
  GPU is used only for a sentence the reader is about to hear that isn't
  buffered yet, and only while the app is open. The bar shows how much is
  ready; skipping back is instant; a relaunch starts from the buffer
  before the model has loaded.
- **Readr Voice keeps reading with the screen locked.** Playback comes
  from the buffer, and the CPU refills it in the background whenever less
  than half an hour is ready, until an hour is. If the buffer runs dry
  with the phone locked, narration pauses at the sentence boundary with
  "Unlock Readr to keep listening" on the lock screen and as a
  notification, and resumes on its own when the app comes back. Never an
  Apple voice.
- **Speed changes keep the buffer.** The audio is made at 1× and the
  player applies the speed, so 1.5× continues the sentence in flight from
  where it is rather than re-synthesizing, and nothing prepared is thrown
  away.

- **No MLX work while the app is locked.** On the owner's iPhone 17 Pro a
  "CPU" synthesis still finalized a Metal stream from the background and
  iOS refused it (an uncatchable abort inside MLX), so CPU continuation is
  off by default (`readrVoice.cpuContinuation` turns it on for measuring a
  future MLX). With the screen locked, playback comes from the read-ahead
  buffer only; when it runs out narration holds and says to unlock.

## Also in 3.3.1

- **OpenRouter offers its whole catalogue.** The OpenRouter card's model
  control is now a row naming the current model and its price; tapping it
  opens a picker with a search field and three sections — Recommended (a
  curated eighteen, from Claude Sonnet 5 and GPT-5.6 Sol down to
  DeepSeek V4 Flash at $0.08 in · $0.17 out per million tokens), Free (the
  `:free` rows), and every text model OpenRouter serves, four hundred and
  some, each with its price and context window. The list comes from
  OpenRouter's own `/api/v1/models`, is kept on disk for a day, and falls
  back to the day-old copy and then to the curated list when the network is
  away (the picker says so). A model picked from the live list keeps its
  real context window across relaunches — the app no longer swaps an
  unfamiliar OpenRouter id for the default.
- **A connected card stops asking you to connect.** With a key stored — or
  a sign-in done — a provider card shows its status, Disconnect, and the
  model control, and nothing else: no Sign in button, no key field, no
  console link. The OpenRouter card used to keep all three over a working
  key and read as not connected. A rejected key brings the field back so
  it can be replaced.
- **Ask's scope is a choice, not a switch.** The "Whole book" toggle in the
  Ask panel read as a feature to turn on. It is now "Answers from" with two
  segments — "Up to where I am" and "Whole book" — and a line beneath
  saying what the chosen one means: "Answers stop where you stopped reading
  — no spoilers." or "Answers may use the whole book, including what you
  haven't read."

## Under the hood

- `NarrationStatus.preparing` (ReadrKit, table-tested): the engine reports
  `isPreparing` for a request it has taken but cannot voice yet, and
  `didBeginSpeaking` when audio starts; a word boundary, a finish or a
  failure end the wait too. Preparing counts as active and as playing (the
  pause control pauses it, `play()` is a no-op, a skip moves on) but not as
  listening (the sleep timer waits with it and resumes when audio starts).
  The silent-engine backstop holds a dropped preparing utterance rather
  than advancing past a sentence nobody heard.
- `NarrationEnginePolicy` has no fallback rows any more: a Readr Voice
  request goes to the Kokoro engine whether or not its model is in, and
  the engine waits; the platform voice reads only non-Readr-Voice ids or —
  the backstop — a Readr Voice request to an engine that has `.failed`,
  which `NarrationModel` never issues without re-preparing first (the
  Retry, and a plain play, both re-prepare a failed engine synchronously
  before the sentence routes). `RoutingSpeechEngine` forwards a Kokoro
  failure as a failure instead of re-speaking it through the Apple voice.
- The lookahead is ReadrKit's (`SpeechPrefetching`, table-tested): when a
  sentence starts, after a skip and after a voice change the controller
  hands an engine that conforms the sentences that follow, built by
  `SpeechPlaylist.upcomingSegments(limit:)` and cut by
  `NarrationController.segments(_:within:rate:)` at the horizon —
  `.seconds(3600)` by default, `.restOfBook` while charging — with the
  estimate at fifteen characters a second scaled by the rate. Every
  request carries `bookID`. A speed-only change goes to
  `SpeechRateAdjusting.adjustRate` first and re-speaks only if the engine
  declines; either way the lookahead is not re-issued for speed.
  `NarrationHoldReason.needsForeground` arrives through `didSuspend`, holds
  narration `.paused` with `holdReason` set, and `play()` re-speaks.
- `ReadrVoiceAudioCache` (App): `Caches/ReadrVoiceAudio/<book>/<voice>/
  <sha256(voice+text)>_<ms>.m4a`, mono AAC at 64kbps written with
  `AVAudioFile` at 1×, an in-memory index from the directory listing, two
  hours across all books with the oldest evicted first, and a per-book
  directory `AppModel.removeBook` deletes. `SentenceAudioPlayer` takes
  `PlayableAudio` (`.nothing`, `.data` for the CoreML engine's WAV,
  `.file(url, rate:)` played with `enableRate`) and `setRate` in place.
- `MLXKokoroSpeechEngine`: `PrefetchDevicePolicy` defaults to
  `.gpuWhileActive` — a cache hit plays without the model (logged with the
  seconds still ahead); a miss in the foreground, playback or prefetch
  alike, synthesizes on the GPU (the sentence being spoken waits out at
  most one graph already on the actor, then goes first, and the wait is
  logged with its duration); a miss with the app backgrounded is a
  `didSuspend` unless a CPU synthesis of that very sentence is in flight
  with a rolling realtime factor of 0.8 or better. `readrVoice.
  prefetchOnCPUOnly` (default off) forces the pump onto the CPU even while
  active, to measure `cpuRealtimeFactor` against a live GPU baseline;
  `readrVoice.prefetchOnGPU` is kept as a no-op alias for the (now
  default) GPU-while-active behaviour. The pump runs one graph at a time —
  to the horizon in the foreground, and in the background only while less
  than 30 minutes is ahead, until 60 is, and only while the CPU hasn't
  been marked unavailable this session — with `Device.withDefaultDevice
  (.cpu)` chosen on the actor immediately before each CPU synthesis (the
  weights still load on the GPU in the foreground only; a CPU warm-up
  follows the load, deferred behind any real sentence the pump still has
  to prefetch so it never races prefetch for the one admitted graph, and
  is deliberately the first CPU use of the session so a CPU that cannot
  synthesize at all fails on a throwaway sentence first). Every CPU
  synthesis now also runs with MLX's JIT compiler globally disabled
  (`mlx_disable_compile`/`mlx_enable_compile`, the `Cmlx` C target — no
  Swift wrapper exists) around exactly the one admitted graph: on-device
  evidence found `[Compiled::eval_cpu] CPU compilation not supported on
  the platform` (mlx-c) inside Kokoro's compiled activation layers
  (MLXNN's `compile(shapeless:)`) on some hardware, an MLX-reported error
  that reached `withError` (below) but would otherwise recur every
  sentence. If a CPU synthesis still throws — compilation disabled or not
  — the session marks the CPU unavailable, logs it at `.warning` with the
  error, stops the pump's CPU refill, and never retries the CPU: playback
  then relies on the buffer alone, holding (the existing
  `didSuspend(.needsForeground)`) when it runs dry rather than reaching
  for a device that keeps failing. The actual graph — the model load, a
  warm-up, and every synthesis — runs inside mlx-swift's scoped
  `withError` and the compute itself runs on a `nonisolated` method, so
  neither an MLX-reported error nor a long CPU compile can trap the
  process or pin the actor against a concurrently arriving playback
  request. Per sentence it records device, synthesis ms and audio ms;
  every sentence, not one in ten, goes to `DiagnosticsLog` at info (the
  file sink caps its size and rotates), and `cpuRealtimeFactor` is the
  rolling ratio over the last five CPU sentences. The synthesis deadline
  is 30s on the GPU and 180s on the CPU. `Memory.cacheLimit` is unchanged.
- `NarrationModel` publishes `secondsAhead` (from
  `secondsBuffered(ahead:)` over the controller's `lookahead`) and
  `holdReason`; sets the horizon from `UIDevice.batteryState` and follows
  its changes; asks for notification permission once, the first time
  Listen is used with Readr Voice where the hold can happen; posts the
  hold as a local notification when the app is not active and puts it on
  the lock screen's second line; and on `willEnterForeground` clears the
  notification and plays. The Listen bar's `listen.ahead` shows the figure
  from a minute up; `listen.hold` shows the reason.
- `OpenRouterModel` / `OpenRouterModelCatalog` / `OpenRouterModelStore`
  (ReadrKit, table-tested): the parse of `GET /api/v1/models` keeps text-
  output models, drops `:batch` variants and `~` router aliases, converts
  per-token prices to per-million and sorts by name; the store is an actor
  with an injectable fetch, `Caches/OpenRouterModels.json` with a 24h TTL
  (stale copy served when a refresh fails, curated list when there is no
  copy), and returns the curated list only under any `-uiTest…` flag.
  `ProviderCatalog.openRouterCurated` is the recommended eighteen with
  offline prices; `openRouterModels` derives from it (budget = min(context,
  200K), no caching); `registerOpenRouterModels` fills a lock-protected
  budget registry that `resolve(modelID:for:)` consults for an OpenRouter id
  outside the static list (128K before the catalogue has loaded) instead of
  falling back to the default. `openai/gpt-5.6` → `openai/gpt-5.6-sol` and
  the retired Llama free row → `minimax/minimax-m3:free` in
  `legacyReplacements`. `AppModel` registers the disk copy at launch;
  `SettingsModel.loadOpenRouterModels()` loads it for the sheet.
- `-uiTestSeedProviderKeys` now seeds OpenRouter as well as Anthropic and
  OpenAI, which is what `testConnectedCardHidesSignInAndKeyField` runs on.
- `MARKETING_VERSION` 3.3.1.
