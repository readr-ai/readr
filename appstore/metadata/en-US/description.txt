Read, listen, and ask questions as you go. Readr answers from what you've
read so far and never spoils what's ahead — and clearly labels anything it
adds from elsewhere.

ASK THE BOOK
Open an EPUB, PDF, plain-text or Markdown file you already own, select a
sentence, and ask about it. Readr answers from the pages you've read, not
the pages you haven't, so nothing ahead of your place can slip out. Choose
"Whole book" to ask about all of it. And it isn't limited to the
book: ask how the science has moved on, who directed the adaptation, or how
an idea aged, and you get a useful answer that shows which part came from
the book and which from outside it. Readr won't attribute an outside claim
to the book you're reading.

Back after a week away? Tap Recap on your Continue Reading card and Readr
tells you the story so far, from only the pages you've read — it can't
spoil what comes next.

Ask needs an AI account of your own: connect an Anthropic, OpenAI or
OpenRouter key, or sign in with OpenRouter. You pay that company directly for
what you use, and you can change or disconnect at any time. On an Apple
silicon Mac you can use a local Ollama model instead and stay offline. Without one, Readr is still a complete reader — Ask simply stays off.

LISTEN
Press Listen and Readr reads the book aloud, starting from the page you're on
— not the top of the chapter. The page turns itself to follow the voice, and
the place it saves is where the voice left off, so switching between reading
and listening never costs you your spot.

The narrator is Readr Voice, a neural voice that runs entirely on your
device: no account, no subscription, and nothing about your book ever leaves
the device to be read aloud. Its voice model downloads once, the first time
you listen, and the Listen card shows the wait. Readr Voice keeps reading with the screen locked: it reads ahead
while the app is open and plays from that with the phone in your pocket.
Prefer Apple's built-in voices? They're a tap away under "Other voices" in
the Aa menu's Voice row, with nothing to download at all.

Skip by sentence, jump chapters from Contents, change the speed or the voice mid-sentence and
it carries on from that word, and set a sleep timer that counts listening
rather than clock time — pause for lunch and the countdown waits for you. It
keeps playing with the screen locked, with lock-screen and headphone controls,
and reads PDFs as well as EPUBs.

HIGHLIGHTS BECOME A DRAFT
Highlight as you read, then turn those highlights into a first draft. Readr
orders them by reading position, keeps your quotes intact, and turns them into
an editable draft you can export as Markdown.

PRIVACY
No usage tracking. No account. No subscription. Your books, highlights, notes and questions
live on your device, and reading them aloud happens there too. When you use
Ask, your question and the book text needed to answer it go straight to the
company you connected — never through a Readr server, because there isn't
one. Your keys are stored in the device Keychain.

READING THAT FEELS LIKE READING
• EPUB, PDF, plain-text and Markdown — DRM-free files you own
• A book to start on: Alice's Adventures in Wonderland is in your library on
  first launch, so you can try everything before importing your own
• Three layouts: continuous scroll, single page, or two facing pages
• Paper, Sepia and Dark themes, with your choice of typeface and spacing
• Table of contents, bookmarks, in-book search, footnote popups
• Five highlight colors, notes, and a Notes panel that exports to Markdown
• Continue Reading picks up exactly where you left off, reading or listening
• On iPad: sidebar and reader side by side, two-page spreads, and hardware
  keyboard page turns

FREE AND OPEN SOURCE
Readr is free and MIT-licensed, developed in the open. Read the code, file an
issue, or send a bug report from inside the app — there's a Report a bug
button in Settings that attaches your app version and recent errors, and
nothing about what you've been reading.
